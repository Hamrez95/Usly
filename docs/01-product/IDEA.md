# Usly — Product Idea

## One-line promise
Every week, Usly helps two partners turn their mood, available time, and budget into one real shared experience.

## Problem
Busy couples often care about their relationship but fail to convert good intentions into shared time. Planning becomes repetitive, one-sided, expensive, or postponed.

## Initial target segment
Working couples aged roughly 25–38 who have been together for at least one year, have limited shared time, and regularly struggle to decide what to do together.

## Jobs to be done
- Help us understand what kind of shared time fits both of us this week.
- Reduce the “what should we do?” decision burden.
- Turn discussion into a concrete, scheduled activity.
- Help us remember which experiences actually made us happy.

## Core loop
1. Each partner answers a three-question weekly sync privately.
2. Usly reveals neutral shared context after both complete it.
3. Usly offers three experience options: easy, balanced, different.
4. Both vote; one experience is selected.
5. The couple schedules and completes it.
6. Each answers one lightweight feedback question.
7. Future suggestions improve from explicit preferences.

## Product principles
- Fun before therapy.
- Action before content consumption.
- Consent before sharing.
- No relationship score, partner score, blame, diagnosis, or automated judgment.
- Useful in solo mode, better in couple mode.
- One couple subscription; never force two separate payments.
- Sensitive content is opt-in and mutually enabled.

## MVP hypothesis
If Usly provides a short weekly ritual and relevant low-friction activity suggestions, both partners will return weekly and complete more intentional shared experiences.

## Main risks
- One partner participates and the other does not.
- Suggestions feel generic or repetitive.
- The product feels like homework or therapy.
- Couples enjoy it but will not pay.
- Sensitive data creates privacy or safety risks.

## Explicit non-goals for MVP
- Couples therapy or crisis intervention
- AI relationship counselor
- Chat or messaging
- Full shared calendar
- Banking, wallet, or expense accounting
- Public social network
- Live location
- Relationship health percentage
- Gamified partner comparison or punitive streaks
